# 6. Stating plainly that the front ends are AI-assisted

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-26

## Context

Several of the author's projects have web front ends, some of which look
professionally built. They were planned and assembled with AI tooling such as
Lovable. The author was explicit:

> most of the frontends you saw were made by me using AI tools such as lovable.
> So do not oversell my frontend skills when actually those sites reflect more my
> abilities to plan and use AI tools instead.

A portfolio that let a reader assume those were hand-built front-end work would
misrepresent him — and would fall apart in a technical interview.

## Decision

The site says it outright, in two places: a standing note on `/open-source`, and a
paragraph on `/about`. Both frame the skill accurately — scoping and directing the
work — rather than either hiding it or apologising for it.

The site's own positioning is backend and infrastructure throughout. Front-end
repositories are not featured on their own; the celestial-mechanics front end is
mentioned only alongside its API.

## Consequences

**Good.** The claims survive scrutiny. Someone who reads the site and then
interviews him finds exactly what was advertised. Directing AI tooling to a
shipped result is a real and currently valuable skill, and stating it plainly
presents it as one.

**Cost.** It forgoes credit a vaguer site could have collected. That is the point.

**This is load-bearing.** Do not quietly drop or soften these notes while editing
copy. If the positioning ever changes, it changes because the author says so.
